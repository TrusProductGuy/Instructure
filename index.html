AI in Assessment

Where I see the highest-leverage AI opportunities in Instructure's Assessment domain.

Dan Meyer — one of edtech's sharpest critical voices — places assessment software in a high-impact, low-usage quadrant. Gallup confirms it: only 32% of teachers use AI at all weekly. AI in education does things faster and cheaper, but not yet enough things better. The strategic question for Instructure's Assessment domain isn't whether to build — it's how to move from the low-usage half of that quadrant to the high-usage half, without throwing what Meyer calls "boxes of loose jet ski parts" at already-overwhelmed teachers.

High Impact → High Usage

Close the gap between assessment data and instructional decision

Mastery Predictive Assessments and Quick Reassess already help teachers act on results — that's real progress. The next frontier is reducing the cognitive distance further: AI that moves from "here are your standards gaps" to "here's a specific instructional sequence for this cohort, this week." That shift — from insight to prescription — is what turns high-impact software into high-usage software. It's also what Meyer's framework says is missing from most edtech AI today.

Delivery Problem

Question authoring works when it reduces teacher burden, not when it adds a review task

Question Authoring Assistance is in Canvas Core. The risk Meyer's critique names precisely: tools that produce output that looks reasonable but still requires significant expert judgment to validate — effectively handing teachers a rough draft with a "your problem now" attached. The product opportunity is quality high enough that teachers spend less time reviewing than they would creating from scratch. Until that threshold is crossed, adoption will stay in "dabbler" territory.

Agency & Governance

AI-assisted grading only scales when teachers stay in control of the decision

Grading assistance is coming to IgniteAI, and the 2026 expert consensus is clear: the institutions that adopt AI confidently are those where teachers retain visible agency over outcomes. Grades affect placement and graduation — the trust model isn't a nice-to-have. Audit trails, calibration controls, and override mechanisms aren't bureaucratic overhead; they're the product features that make the difference between a tool that gets adopted district-wide and one that gets quietly disabled after a parent complaint.

Integrity Architecture

LLM-enabled assignments as authentic assessment — and the integrity answer

Instructure's LLM-enabled assignment embeds a structured AI conversation directly in the learning experience, capturing student reasoning as learning evidence. The strategic upside beyond pedagogy: it's the most credible response to the academic integrity problem. Brookings recently labeled unsanctioned student AI use "the fast food of education" — cheap, ubiquitous, and nutritionally empty. When AI interaction is the assignment — designed by the instructor, happening inside Canvas, with reasoning captured and graded — you replace the integrity arms race with an integrity architecture. That's not just a product feature. It's a category-defining answer to the most urgent question in higher ed right now, and a story worth leading every sales conversation with.
